Your legal AI is still citing a law India repealed.

On 1 July 2024 the Indian Penal Code, the Code of Criminal Procedure and the Evidence Act were replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, the Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and the Sakshya Adhiniyam. Almost every section got a new number. Language models learned the old ones. Worse, the old numbers still exist in the new codes with different meanings: ask for the murder section and a model may answer “BNS 302”, which is the offence of wounding religious feelings.

Experiment · LoRA fine-tuning on the mapping

Can you simply retrain the model? Not really.

We fine-tuned Qwen3-1.7B (LoRA, 5,805 examples) on the old→new mapping for 80% of sections, using different wording from the benchmark. The question is whether that knowledge transfers to the 20% of sections it never saw, and what else breaks.

Most old-code citations by courts are correct: offences committed before 1 July 2024 are still tried under the IPC. That is why sanhita doesn't just say “wrong”; it says when each code applies.

Under the hood

How sanhita reads a citation

  1. Parse. A citation grammar for how Indians actually write law: u/s 302 r/w 34 IPC, Sections 447, 506 read with Section 34, S. 161(2) Cr.P.C., धारा 420 भारतीय दंड संहिता. Measured on real citation strings from Supreme Court judgments: .
  2. Map. Every old section → its new section(s) via NCRB's official correspondence tables, including splits (498A → BNS 85 + 86) and omissions (124A sedition).
  3. Detect carry-over. If a new-code section is cited but the surrounding words match the old section with that number far better, it's the classic carry-over error.
  4. Respect time. An IPC citation for a 2023 offence is correct; a BNS citation for it is not.

sanhita "Murder is punishable under BNS 302."      # → CARRYOVER: you mean BNS 103
sanhita --map IPC 420                             # → BNS 318  Cheating
